    Building the Harvesthold¶

    Every permanent improvement in Chosen of the Harvest is a sanctum: a billboard placed on a one-metre grid around the hub altar. Sanctums produce food, wood, stone, herbs, grace and pearls; they house workers and sleepers; and their auras quiet or radicalise the companions standing nearby. This page covers the build menu, placement rules, all twenty sanctum types, the condition and dirt model, worker hauling, and the small interior grid you can decorate.

    Two keys, one system

    B opens the build menu in the hub and the interior menu inside a building. K opens the cross-system catalog, which lists every sanctum with its cost and effect without entering placement mode.

    Placing a sanctum¶

    ShowBuildMenu pages through SanctumType five entries at a time; choosing one calls BeginPlacement. Placement is a separate input mode: the game locks movement, spawns a translucent ghost, and waits for you to commit.

    Input Action
    W A S D Move the ghost one metre at a time
    Q Rotate a quarter turn (cosmetic flip)
    E or Space Confirm
    Esc Cancel and refund nothing, because nothing was spent

    The ghost is green when the spot is legal and affordable, red otherwise. Four rules decide legality:

    if (Mathf.Abs(position.x) > hubBounds.x - 1.5f ||
    Mathf.Abs(position.z) > hubBounds.y - 1.5f)
    return false;

foreach (var building in buildings)
{
    if (building == null || building == ignore) continue;
    if (Vector3.Distance(position, building.transform.position) < 2.4f) return false;
}

    The remaining checks reject the statue, altar, portal, NPC, gathering nodes and the player's own body. Moving an existing sanctum uses the same ghost and the same checks, but skips the affordability test — relocating is free, and workers and sleepers are re-bound to the new spot on confirmation.

    The hub box is tighter than the walkable box

    You can walk to about ±19 by ±14, but placement is limited to the hub bounds minus 1.5 m. A sanctum also keeps 2.4 m of clear ground around it, so a dense row of farms is not possible. Plan lanes, not clusters.

    The twenty sanctums¶

    Costs are charged in wood and stone at placement. Aura is the radius in metres inside which the building's continuous effect applies; — means no aura.

    The label you see is localised, so the herb garden is "Herb Terrace", the farm is "Rose Garden" and the temple is the "Grand Temple" in English. Sanctum.Label looks up building.<type> in the lexicon and falls back to the raw enum name.

    Production, in detail¶

    Production is timer-based and checked in Sanctum.Update. Each cycle adds an amount to the building's internal Stored buffer, which you collect by pressing R on the building. The buffer's capacity is:

    int capacity = Capacity + bonus * 4 + levelBonus * 4;

    Capacity is 10, bonus is the worker's WorkBonus and levelBonus is Level - 1. A level-3 building with a skilled worker therefore buffers 22 units before it stops producing — enough to leave running while you are in the Fallow.

    Sanctum Interval Base yield Per worker point Per level
    Farm 12 s 2 food +1 +1
    Lumberyard 10 s 1 wood +1 +1
    Quarry 12 s 1 stone +1 +1
    Herb garden 14 s 1 herb +1 +1
    Sawmill 9 s 2 wood +1 +1
    Stoneworks 10 s 2 stone +1 +1
    Wheat field 10 s 2 food +1 +1
    Shrine 8 s 1 grace +1 +1
    Kitchen 16 s 2 food → 1 meal — —
    Apothecary 18 s 2 herbs → 1 medicine — —
    Obelisk 12 s 1 grace — —
    Temple 12 s 2 grace — —
    Bell tower 15 s 1 grace — —
    Reliquary 18 s 1 pearl — —

    Shrines and the four grace buildings are the exception to the Stored model: they credit grace directly and pop a floating "+1 grace" over the roof, so there is nothing to collect. Farms and wheat fields add HarvestState.BonusFood at collection time, which is how the Harvest creed and the Wheel omen turn a 2-food cycle into a 3-food cycle.

    Workers are multipliers, not requirements

    Only the kitchen and apothecary refuse to run without a worker. Every other producer keeps ticking at its base rate, so an unstaffed farm is slow, not dead. Assigning a worker to a farm is usually a better return than building a second farm, because the worker adds to yield and capacity.

    Condition and dirt¶

    Sanctums wear out. Update subtracts 0.04 condition per second from every building, which is 100 condition in roughly 42 minutes of real time. Condition scales output linearly from 0.55× at zero to 1× at full:

    float conditionScale = Mathf.Lerp(0.55f, 1f, Condition / 100f);

    Nothing stops producing at zero condition; it simply produces at 55%. Repair is priced from the missing condition, not the building's cost:

    Cost Formula
    Wood max(1, ceil((100 − condition) × 0.12))
    Stone max(1, ceil((100 − condition) × 0.08))

    A building at 50% costs 6 wood and 4 stone; a building at 12% costs 11 wood and 8 stone. Repair always restores to 100, never partially, and the menu refuses when condition is already above 99.5.

    Shrines have a second axis. They accumulate dirt at 0.25 per second, and grace output is multiplied by Mathf.Lerp(1f, 0.35f, Dirt / 100f) — a fully filthy shrine produces about a third of its listed grace. Companions on cleaning duty scrub 12 dirt per second, and they will pick a shrine automatically when it is the dirtiest thing in the hold.

    Managing a sanctum¶

    Pressing R at a building opens a six-entry menu. The header shows its label, level and condition.

    Entry Cost Result
    Move Free Re-enters placement with the building hidden
    Upgrade round(cost × 0.6) + 10 wood, round(cost × 0.6) + 8 stone +1 level, max 3
    Remove Refunds half the base wood and stone Deletes the sanctum and unassigns staff
    Collect / Craft / Stores / Release Free Context action for that type
    Repair See above Restores condition to 100
    Enter interior Free Switches to the 7 × 5 interior grid

    Upgrading is intentionally cheaper than rebuilding: a Farm upgrade costs 22 wood and 8 stone, while a second Farm costs 20 wood and occupies new ground. The trade is space against throughput, and it is the main reason vertical specialisation beats sprawl in a mature hold.

    Removing a building is not destructive to companions. RemoveSanctum releases any penitent, clears the worker's job, unassigns the sleeper, refunds half the cost, and then re-runs worker and bed assignment for the whole roster.

    Workers, sleepers and hauling¶

    TryAssignWorker walks the companion list and gives an unemployed, able companion the first job that needs one. The NeedsWorker set is farm, lumberyard, quarry, shrine, kitchen, herb garden, apothecary, sawmill, stoneworks and wheat field. A companion cannot work while sick, dissenting or confined.

    Production is not the only labour. A worker whose buffer reaches four units (two for an herb garden) picks the load up and carries it to the hold's deposit point:

    if (Job != null && Job.Stored >= (Job.Type == SanctumType.HerbGarden ? 2 : 4))
    StartHaul();

    HaulSpot is the storage building's front edge if one exists, otherwise the altar. On arrival, DepositHaul credits the resources and the worker walks back. FlushHaul deposits anything in hand before a save or a forced task change, so resources are never silently lost when you reassign someone mid-carry.

    Storage and caps¶

    Storage is the only building whose effect is global. Each level adds to every cap, and HarvestState.NotifyChanged clamps all eight resources after every change, so overflow is impossible.

    Resource Base cap Per storage level
    Wood 250 +100
    Stone 250 +100
    Food 100 +50
    Herbs 30 +20
    Meals 30 +20
    Medicine 10 +5
    Bombs 5 +2
    Potions 20 +0

    Bombs also gain +2 from the Magician omen and +2 from the Lantern heirloom; potions gain +5 from the Hermit omen. Those bonuses apply whether or not you own a storage, but the storage multiplier is what makes a bomb-heavy run practical.

    Bell tower, temple and the loyalty auras¶

    Auras are checked once per frame per companion in FollowerAgent. They are small, continuous drips rather than bursts, and they stack only in the sense that a companion standing in two auras gets both.

    Building Per-second effect inside the aura
    Shrine +0.018 loyalty
    Tea house +0.01 loyalty
    Sun bath +0.01 loyalty, −0.08 exhaustion
    Obelisk Loyalty aura, plus grace production
    Temple Loyalty aura, plus grace production
    Bell tower Loyalty aura, plus grace production
    Reliquary Small loyalty aura, plus pearls

    The Tea House is the social building: its 5.2 m radius lets companions find conversation partners farther apart, which is what drives friendship and romance checks. The Sun Bath is the recovery building: it burns exhaustion while adding loyalty, making it the pair to a workshop district.

    Interiors¶

    Choosing Enter interior hides the hub and builds a small room around you. The floor is 8.2 m across, movement is bounded to ±3.6 by ±3.1, and the camera pulls in to 8.2. The buildable grid is 7 columns by 5 rows:

    static Vector2Int InteriorCell(Vector3 position) =>
    new Vector2Int(
        Mathf.Clamp(Mathf.RoundToInt(position.x), -3, 3),
        Mathf.Clamp(Mathf.RoundToInt(position.z), -2, 2));

    Pieces are placed on the cell you are standing on, and an occupied cell is refused. Costs are deliberately small:

    Piece Cost Notes
    Wall 5 wood Solid; blocks movement
    Door 10 wood Walk-through, the exit prompt
    Candle 3 pearls Decoration; +0.6 aura to the parent sanctum

    Each candle permanently raises InteriorAuraBonus on the sanctum by 0.6 m, and removing one lowers it again. That is the only way to grow an aura beyond its base radius, and it applies to whichever aura the building already has — a bell tower with three candles projects 8.8 m instead of 7.

    Interiors are saved, not rebuilt from a template

    Every piece is written into SanctumData.interior as a type, x, z triple. BuildInterior replays that list on load. Removing a piece removes its entry and its aura bonus, so a save always matches what you see.

    Under the hood¶

    Sanctum (Scripts/Buildings/Building.cs) is a plain MonoBehaviour with no ScriptableObject backing. The type, level, stored amount, dirt, condition and three companion references are public fields; costs and aura radii are static switch expressions on the type.

    HarvestGame owns the list of placed sanctums and mirrors it into HarvestState.Data.buildings as SanctumData records for saving. The two lists are index-aligned, which is why moving or removing a building has to update both in the same method — see ConfirmPlacement and RemoveSanctum.

    Placement validity is deliberately geometric rather than physics-based:

    bool geometry = IsPlacementValid(placementType, placementCursor, placementMoving);
bool affordable = placementMoving != null ||
                  (HarvestState.Data.wood >= Sanctum.CostWood(placementType) &&
                   HarvestState.Data.stone >= Sanctum.CostStone(placementType));

    There is no overlap test against gathering nodes or companions inside IsPlacementValid itself; those checks run earlier in the same method, before the distance loop, so keep them together if you extend the rule set.

    RebuildNavGrid runs after every placement, move and removal. Companions use a coarse grid rather than a navmesh, so a wall placed across the middle of the hold can genuinely trap someone until the next rebuild — which is exactly what happens, because the rebuild is triggered by the placement, not by the trapped companion.
