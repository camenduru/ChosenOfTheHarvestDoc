    Expeditions into the Fallow¶

    The Fallow is the template's roguelike half. It is entered through the portal in the Harvesthold, it is generated one room at a time from a fixed act budget, and it is the only place where pearls, omens and heirlooms come from. This page covers the curse roll, the floor graph, every room type, keys and corridors, the act pacing, and how a run ends.

    A run is a snapshot

    The Fallow is not a separate scene. EnterExpedition disables hubRoot, resets your health, rolls a curse, and hands control to Expedition.Begin. When you leave, ExitExpedition re-enables the hub at the exact position you left from — building timers and companion needs resume where they stopped.

    Entering the portal¶

    Standing at the portal and pressing E opens ShowCampaignMap(), not the dungeon. The map lists three acts plus Devotion upgrades and Quest log. An act that is ahead of campaignStage is refused with "Clear the previous warden first". Choosing an available act shows an intro card, and only its Begin pilgrimage entry calls:

    public void EnterExpedition(int act)
{
    if (mode != Mode.Hub || ceremonyRunning) return;

    currentAct = Mathf.Clamp(act, 0, 2);
    HarvestState.RollCurse();
    mode = Mode.Expedition;
    hubReturnPosition = Player.transform.position;
    hubRoot.SetActive(false);

    Audio.Play("portal", 0.8f);
    Audio.PlayMusic("music_dungeon");
    ...
    HarvestState.Data.pilgrimages++;
    Player.SetHealth(Player.MaxHealth);
    ExpeditionRef.Begin(this, currentAct);
}

    pilgrimages is not cosmetic: it is the depth scalar used by chest pearls, trap damage, and every husk's health and damage formula.

    Curses rolled on entry¶

    HarvestState.RollCurse() picks uniformly from the three real curses — it can never return Curse.None:

    Curse Effect Where it is applied
    Fury +30% enemy damage Creature.DamageMultiplier at spawn
    Frailty −25 maximum health RunHealthPenalty, subtracted in MaxHealth
    Gloom +25% enemy speed Creature.SpeedMultiplier at spawn

    The curse is runtime-only (HarvestState.CurrentCurse) and is cleared by ClearRunState() on exit, so it never leaks into a save. It is printed in the HUD status line next to the husk count.

    Act structure¶

    Three acts, two floors each, six floors in total. Each floor is regenerated from scratch, so the graph is different every descent.

    Act Biome Warden Floor grid Rooms per floor Rooms per act
    0 Ashen Orchard The Gilded Stag 5 × 2 10 20
    1 Drowned Conservatory The Mire Queen 8 × 2 16 32
    2 Ember Citadel The Obsidian Sultan 5 × 4 20 40

    JourneyPacing (Scripts/Core/CampaignPacing.cs) states the same budget as constants and adds the intended session length:

    Constant Value
    ActOneRooms 20
    ActTwoRooms 32
    ActThreeRooms 40
    TotalRooms 92
    TargetMinutesMin / Max 80 / 120
    Summary 92 rooms • 80-120 min target

    The warden reward budget is 8 / 12 / 16 devotion, which WardenDefeated computes as 8 + act * 4 before the crown heirloom multiplier.

    How a floor is generated¶

    FloorPlanGenerator.Generate(act, out lockedEdges) builds the whole graph before the first room is drawn. It lays out a rectangular grid, connects every room to its right and lower neighbour (so every room is reachable), marks rooms[0] as the entrance and the last room as the warden, then distributes role rooms into the middle.

    int columns = act switch { 1 => 8, 2 => 5, _ => 5 };
int rows = act == 2 ? 4 : 2;
int bossId = columns * rows - 1;
...
var roles = new List<RoomType>
{
    RoomType.Shop, RoomType.Treasure, RoomType.Curse,
    RoomType.Puzzle, RoomType.Rescue, RoomType.Forge
};
int elites = 1 + act;
for (int i = 0; i < elites; i++) roles.Add(RoomType.Elite);
if (act >= 1) roles.Add(RoomType.Treasure);

    The role list is shuffled, then written into consecutive room ids from 1 upwards; anything left before the warden becomes a plain Combat room. That guarantees every utility room appears at least once per floor, with one elite in act one, two in act two and three in act three, plus a second treasure room from act two.

    Finally it locks Mathf.Min(2 + act, candidates.Count) edges between ordinary rooms — never an edge touching the entrance or the warden — and returns them as packed EdgeKey(a, b) integers. Those become the locked doors.

    Room types¶

    RoomType has ten members. FloorPlanGenerator.Name localises them through the room.* lexicon keys.

    Room Objective What is inside
    Entrance none Spawn room; doors open immediately
    Combat clear the room 4 husks, doors open at zero
    Elite defeat the elite 5 husks, one carries an elite modifier, 3 traps
    Treasure open the chest Chest gives pearls, wood, stone and a key
    Shop trade, then choose a door Merchant with limited stock and a sell menu
    Curse touch the altar Take the gift: +1 key, +25 pearls, −20 health
    Puzzle step on 3 runes Three RunePlate triggers; all three open the doors
    Rescue free the captive RecruitCompanion(), blocked at the companion cap
    Forge use the forge Sharpen blade for 15 pearls (+5 damage)
    Warden slay the warden Boss + two escorts, then a boss chest

    Expedition.Update watches Creature.All.Count for combat and elite rooms and calls OpenDoors() the moment the room is empty. There is no wave logic — a summoner that keeps adding minions keeps the room open.

    Temperance

    The temperance omen heals 8 health on every cleared room except the entrance, and the toast changes to say so. It is one of the strongest sustain picks in the deck; see Ceremonies and omens.

    Keys, doors and corridors¶

    Keys are a per-floor counter, reset to 0 on every descent. There are three sources: a treasure chest (+1), the merchant (10 pearls), and the cursed altar (+1). Each locked door costs exactly one key and stays unlocked afterwards.

    Doors behave in a strict order:

    1. If the room is not cleared, the door refuses and prints the objective.
    2. If the door is locked and you have a key, it consumes the key, recolours itself lilac and stays in place — you must press again.
    3. Otherwise it builds a corridor and moves you into the target room.

    A corridor is its own tiny room: RoomHalfWidth = 3.2, RoomHalfDepth = 5.5, no enemies, no chest, one door at the far end. The label above that door names the room type you are about to enter, which is the only preview the game gives you before committing.

    The pilgrimage map and campaign stages¶

    CampaignStage is Data.campaignStage clamped to 0–3. It is the number of wardens you have broken, and it gates the act list: act 0 is available at stage 0, act 1 at stage 1, act 2 at stage 2. The map also exposes the devotion screen and the quest log.

    WardenDefeated(act, bossName) only pays out the first time an act's warden falls:

    if (act + 1 > HarvestState.Data.campaignStage)
{
    HarvestState.Data.campaignStage = act + 1;
    int reward = Mathf.RoundToInt((8 + act * 4) *
        (HarvestState.HasHeirloom("crown") ? 1.15f : 1f));
    HarvestState.Data.devotion += reward;
    ...
}

    It is called from Expedition.Update only when the warden dies on the last floor (!HasNextFloor), so a first-floor warden kill does not advance the campaign.

    Descending and finishing¶

    A warden room is the last room of a floor. Killing the warden opens a chest; the chest's omen choice then opens a door labelled DESCEND (or RETURN on the final floor). ExitRun() decides:

    Condition Result
    HasNextFloor BeginNextFloor(): floor++, keys reset, new graph, heal 20% max health
    last floor Finished = true, boot.ExitExpedition(true)

    FloorCount is fixed at 2. The second-floor warden is named with the Ascendant suffix (WardenName appends it when Floor > 1), gets +90 health and +4 damage from floorBonus, and its arena is the same theme as the first floor.

    Leaving with loot autosaves if autosave is enabled, and if campaignStage >= 3 and the victory screen has not been seen, ShowVictory() runs once and grants +10 devotion.

    Dying in the Fallow¶

    OnPlayerDied in the expedition branch takes half your pearls, exits the run with loot = false, and wakes you at max(40, MaxHealth / 2). There is no confiscation of wood, stone, heirlooms or omens — pearls are the only death tax.

    Non-treasure chest the same, then an omen offer of two unowned cards
    Merchant heirloom 25 pearls, attire 30 pearls, potion 18, key 10, heal 30 hp for 15
    Cursed altar +1 key, +25 pearls, −20 health

    Every omen already owned the omen offer converts to +25 pearls

    Shop rooms¶

    ShowShop builds a six-entry merchant menu with per-room stock that resets on every room build: 3 heals, 2 keys, 1 heirloom, 2 potions, 1 attire. The heirloom and attire offers are rolled from RandomUnowned() at room build time, so a shop can never sell you something you already own — and shows "no heirloom to sell" when the catalogue is exhausted. The sell menu trades 10 wood or stone for 8 pearls, 5 food for 6, and 2 herbs for 5.

    Under the hood¶

    The run starts in Expedition.Begin, which clamps the act, fixes the floor count and generates the first graph:

    public void Begin(HarvestGame owner, int act)
{
    ActIndex = Mathf.Clamp(act, 0, 2);
    Floor = 1;
    FloorCount = 2;
    BeginRun(owner);
}

    Only one room exists at a time. EnterRoom destroys the previous roomRoot, despawns every Creature, resets per-room flags (shopUsed, curseUsed, chest/door/exit state), and rebuilds from the room type. Expedition.End is the mirror image: it despawns enemies, destroys the room and clears RoomCollision, which is why leaving mid-room is safe.

    Changing the run length

    FloorCount = 2 and the three JourneyPacing room constants are the only places the 6-floor / 92-room budget is expressed. Changing the grid sizes in FloorPlanGenerator.Generate changes the actual rooms per floor, so update both if you want the HUD and the pacing summary to agree.
